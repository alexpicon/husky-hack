**STOP THE SCAM BEFORE THE MONEY MOVES**

Build a practical intervention that protects people when they are most vulnerable.

**YOUR CHALLENGE**

How might Sound Credit Union recognize when a member (consumer) is being manipulated, compromised, or deceived and deliver the right intervention before the money is lost?

**WHAT YOU WILL DESIGN**

Create a member-facing, employee-facing, or combined solution that addresses authorized scams, account takeover, or both. Your idea may focus on prevention, real-time intervention, trusted contacts, employee escalation, or rapid recovery.

* **Authorized scam:** the member is tricked into sending money themselves.
* **Account takeover (ATO):** a criminal gets into the member’s account and moves money without permission.
  + The difference matters: regulations generally protect members from unauthorized electronic transfers, but usually not from payments they authorized, even under deception, and wires follow separate rules. Authorized scams therefore leave members most exposed.
* Recognize a meaningful moment of risk.
* Intervene before funds become difficult to recover.
* Protect dignity, autonomy, accessibility, and trust.
* Work within Sound’s realistic capabilities (see Capability Assumptions in the Starter Kit).
* Show what happens when the solution is right and when it is wrong, including a legitimate member it flags and a scam it misses.

**AVAILABLE TO YOUR TEAM**

* A student starter kit with fictional scam scenarios, synthetic event data, and a Member Key that includes 20 legitimate members whose activity looks risky.
* Payment amount, timing, channel, rail, status, new-recipient indicators, recent profile changes, password-reset events, held-payment status, balances, history comparisons, and trusted-contact enrollment, as provided in the synthetic data.
* Mobile, online banking, branch, contact-center, notification, human-review, case-escalation, trusted-contact, and recovery concepts.

**DO NOT ASSUME ACCESS TO**

* Device intelligence, behavioral biometrics, or voice-cloning detection.
* Voice analysis, telecom-carrier data, or third-party fraud-consortium feeds.
* A member’s personal calls, texts, email, browser history, social media, or device activity.
* Perfect knowledge that a transaction is fraudulent.

**REQUIRED SUBMISSION**

* Clickable prototype or more functional demonstration.
* Clear member story and scam or takeover journey.
* The trigger, intervention, data used, and role of people.
* False-positive path and member appeal or override experience.
* Realistic 90-day pilot concept and measurable outcomes, with baseline and target assumptions stated (do not use the synthetic data to claim real fraud rates).
* Privacy, accessibility, and responsible-design considerations.
* A walk-through of your trigger on at least one anchor persona (M1001–M1004) and one legitimate look-alike member from the data.
* Format:
  + Phase 1 (Saturday, October 17): 2 minute elevator pitch with 3 minutes of Q&A.
  + Phase 2 (Sunday, October 18): 5-minute live demo + 3-minute Q&A; up to 10 slides; prototype link due by deadline.

**What judges look for:**

**Impact on Member Safety (30%)**

* Prevents or reduces fraud losses
* Intervenes early enough to make a difference
* Includes recovery or member support considerations

**User Experience & Trust (25%)**

* Easy for legitimate members to complete transactions
* Clear, accessible, and non-judgmental
* Works for diverse member needs and abilities

**Feasibility (20%)**

* Realistic to implement
* Uses available data and technology
* Could be piloted by a credit union with reasonable effort

**Innovation (15%)**

* Creative approach beyond standard warnings or fraud scores
* Introduces a new way to protect members

**Prototype & Presentation (10%)**

* Clickable or visual demonstration
* Shows how both members and employees interact with the solution
* Clearly communicates the concept

Ties are broken by Member Harm Prevention, then Trust & Accessibility.

**GUARDRAILS**

Use only synthetic or challenge-provided data. Do not submit only an awareness campaign or fraud score. Trusted-contact features must be voluntary, revocable, and member-directed. A trusted contact is informed, never given account access, and designs should allow for the possibility that the person exploiting a member is a relative or caregiver. Do not restrict members solely because of age or another demographic trait.

**THE GOAL**

Develop a fintech tool that protects members from scams, fraud, and identity theft by helping them identify threats and safeguard their financial accounts and personal information.
